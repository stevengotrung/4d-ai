# The 4D Framework

The [original framework](https://4dframework.org/) describes four observable competencies for working with AI. Its English names are **Define, Dialogue, Discern, Determine Impact**.

| Competency | Core question | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Define | What is worth doing? | Identify who faces the problem, the decision needed, and what success looks like. |
| Dialogue | Who should we ask, and what should we delegate to AI? | Clarify needs with people involved; give AI context, constraints, and output criteria. Keep human responsibility explicit. |
| Discern | Is the result trustworthy? | Check assumptions, weigh evidence, and compare with original records or relevant expertise. |
| Determine Impact | Does it work in practice? | Measure user outcomes, examine trade-offs, and adjust the decision. |

Professional experience underpins all four. Discern is not simply “decide”: decisions occur throughout the framework. Its distinctive focus is whether evidence warrants trusting a result, including recognition of uncertainty.

The competencies form a learning loop. If an output meets requirements but the problem persists, revisit goals, assumptions, and measurement. A measurement plan is not evidence of actual impact.

## Proficiency is separate from workflow completion

The original model uses M1 (with guidance), M2 (independent practice), and M3 (handling complex situations), progressing through autonomy and decision complexity. This skill does not automatically score people. A strong AI-generated artifact does not demonstrate the user's independent competency. If assessment is explicitly requested, use observed human behavior, distinguish AI assistance, and record “insufficient evidence” where appropriate.

The model is a proposal requiring testing across roles. It draws inspiration from SFIA's autonomy/responsibility progression and NIST's consideration of AI trustworthiness; it is not a certification from either organization.
