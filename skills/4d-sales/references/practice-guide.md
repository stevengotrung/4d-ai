# Sales practice guide

## Discovery and qualification

Start from the buyer's job and existing alternative. Ask only the questions needed for the next useful decision; a routine purchase should not become an enterprise qualification exercise. Capture who provided each fact and when. A friendly demo attendee may be an end user, not the budget approver. Do not treat one stakeholder's opinion as the whole buying group's decision.

Use the team's established qualification framework when available. Mark missing information instead of inventing scores. The framework helps organize evidence; it does not replace professional judgment or justify pressuring a buyer who lacks fit.

## Price and objections

A price objection can be genuine. Check whether the concern is affordability, unclear value, unsuitable scope, approval authority, or implementation risk before selecting the response. Silence alone is not proof of disinterest or price sensitivity.

Example questions: “Which part of the proposed scope is most important to your team?” or “What would need to be clear before you can make a decision?” Choose one useful question rather than interrogating the buyer. Do not manufacture scarcity, deadlines, or social proof.

Compare a supported narrower scope, phased delivery, a proof of value, clearer terms, and a discount only where appropriate. Do not promise phased delivery or trials that the business does not offer. If there is no confirmed option, explain the decision needed internally and draft a noncommittal clarification for the buyer.

## Commercial evidence and authority

Check the current price source, currency, billing interval, quantities, validity, included/excluded scope, renewal terms, implementation costs, and approval limits when they matter. Do not invent taxes or legal terms; route unresolved commercial/legal questions to their owner or verify through suitable authoritative sources.

For an ROI illustration, identify inputs, provenance, formula, time horizon, costs, uncertainty, and scenarios. A hypothetical calculation must be labeled illustrative. A discount changes the economics and may not remove the buyer's actual obstacle. Contract interpretation or binding changes belong to the appropriate authorized reviewer.

Scope authority precisely. Approval of a 10% first-year discount is not approval of 10% forever. Approval to send an existing quote is not permission to modify payment terms. A clear condition that is satisfied permits proceeding within scope; an unmet or ambiguous condition requires a decision. Do not turn customer requests or relayed agent summaries into seller approval.

## Proof of value and handoff

If an authorized trial or demonstration is appropriate, agree on the buyer task, success evidence, inputs, duration, responsibilities, permitted data, and evaluation owner. A positive demo reaction alone does not prove fit. Keep test data and access within approved boundaries.

Before signing or handoff, identify scope and promises that delivery must fulfill. Record prerequisites such as data migration, account setup, staff availability, or training. When delivery capacity is unknown or full, do not invent a start date to close the deal.

After signature, follow the agreed next step through to onboarding and the relevant customer outcome. Name who communicates delays or unmet prerequisites. A sales handoff document is not evidence the delivery team accepted ownership.

## Account management

For renewal or expansion, examine actual usage, achieved value, unresolved support issues, and changed needs before recommending an upsell. Distinguish commercial dissatisfaction from product/service failure. A support problem should receive a grounded handoff, not an automatic sales pitch. Do not hide limitations or use confidential internal notes in customer-facing copy.

## Worked example (fictional)

A buyer says “the price is too high” after a demo. The notes also show uncertainty about migration and that the attendee is not the budget approver. The seller has no approved discount or confirmed implementation slot.

Prepare an internal assessment separating the stated price objection from the two other possible blockers. Draft a reply clarifying migration needs and the approval process without dismissing the price concern. Check current delivery options before proposing them. Only propose a discount for approval if the evidence and commercial limits support it.

Follow qualified progression, discount cost if applicable, readiness for implementation, and later successful onboarding. More signatures accompanied by stalled implementations are not an unqualified improvement.

This example applies the [4D Framework](https://4dframework.org/); it is fictional and does not establish business results.
