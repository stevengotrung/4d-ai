# Handoff and closure

Create a handoff only when another team has needed knowledge, authority, or execution responsibility. Include the customer goal, expected and observed state, verified evidence with source pointers, attempts already made, impact, unknowns, the specific decision/action requested, and who will update the customer. Include personal data only in permitted systems when necessary.

Do not send an internal diagnostic note verbatim to the customer. Customer communication should describe the practical next step and known constraints. Engineering acceptance, a refund submission, or a booking proposal is an intermediate state, not customer resolution.

Draft by default. If sending is authorized, verify recipient/channel, approved content, and applicable conditions. Record delivery status supported by the tool result. If a send times out, inspect the conversation or delivery record before retrying. Never claim sent merely because a draft was saved.

Where policy grants scoped routine sending, use it without repeatedly asking, but verify that the case fits its scope. Escalations, commercial commitments, or account changes may need separate authority. A label assigned by the agent itself does not create approval.

For follow-up, record the next owner, date or event trigger, verification procedure, and result location. Distinguish:

- Draft prepared / approved: no message sent yet.
- Sent: dispatch confirmed, not resolution confirmed.
- Awaiting customer / internal action: dependency and owner visible.
- Outcome confirmed: customer confirmation or reliable evidence of the intended result.
- Administratively closed: policy-driven closure, with outcome still unconfirmed when appropriate.

When contacting a reporter after a change, explain what changed, how to use it, prerequisites, and what to do if the issue persists. Prepare a draft if sending is not authorized. Do not create recurring automations or reserve shared resources while waiting unless specifically requested and supported.
