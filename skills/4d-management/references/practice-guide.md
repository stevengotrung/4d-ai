# Management & Team Coordination: practice guide

## Decision records

Record the actual decision-maker, relevant exact approval words/source, scope and conditions, options considered, and why the choice follows the evidence. Separate approved decisions, provisional recommendations, and unresolved questions. If an existing decision already covers the task, continue from it without restarting an approval ritual.

## Coordination without invented consensus

Use proposed owners until acceptance or established authority supports assignment. A meeting invitation is not commitment; silence is not agreement. Clarify dependency direction and what each party needs to proceed. Contact and scheduling require actual authority, not simply a connected calendar or messaging tool.

## Follow-through

Name the next action, owner, due date or trigger, expected evidence, and where it will be recorded. Report blockers without attributing motives to people. Verify the outcome behind a closed task. For a consequential change of scope, timing, or external commitment, bring a concrete revised decision to the authorized owner.

## Worked example (fictional)

**Input:** Sales has requested a launch next month while delivery reports no available capacity. It is unclear whether the date was promised externally or is an internal target.

**Response:** Check the source and authority of the date and the evidence behind capacity. Compare a smaller launch, changed sequencing, or revised timing; clarify dependencies and customer commitments. Prepare a decision brief rather than announcing a plan or assigning people.

**Draft artifact:** Decision owner: to be confirmed from existing governance. Options: reduce scope, revise sequencing, or move target date. Unknown: external promise and validated capacity. Proposed next step: resolve those two facts, then choose an option with explicit trade-offs. Owners are proposed, not assigned.

**Follow-up:** Review accepted ownership, actual throughput and customer/delivery outcomes at the agreed checkpoint. If tasks are closed but the constraint persists, revisit scope and assumptions rather than declaring the launch problem solved.

This example applies the [4D Framework](https://4dframework.org/), a proposed competency model. It is fictional, not evidence of verified results or professional certification.
