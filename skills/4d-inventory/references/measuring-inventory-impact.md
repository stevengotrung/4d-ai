# Measuring inventory impact

A brief is a prediction. The review checks it against what happened on both sides of the trade.

## Every brief names

- **Outcome:** the measure on the side the decision aimed at. Sold-out days per 30 days for Availability; slow-stock value or days of inventory for Cash.
- **Guardrail:** the measure on the opposite side, with its limit. A stockout fix is watched for days of inventory going over the slow-stock flag; a cash fix is watched for new sold-out days.
- **Baseline:** the same measure over a comparable window before the change.
- **Review date:** a calendar date, not "later". For a reorder, set it after the order lands plus the review window, never on the arrival day, because the outcome is measured after arrival.
- **Rules:** what result means keep, adjust or stop.

## Window

Review after 30 days by default. For items selling fewer than about 20 units a month, use 60–90 days, because a month holds too few sales to tell a change from noise. When the window still holds too few sales, the result is *inconclusive*, and that is a valid answer. Say what would make it conclusive.

## Re-scoring

When the merchant comes back with an earlier brief:

1. Read its baseline, outcome, guardrail and rules.
2. Measure the outcome and the guardrail over the review window.
3. Compare like with like: the same weeks of the season, the same channels.
4. Note concurrent changes: promotions, price changes, new channels, changed settings, supplier changes. A before/after difference alone does not prove the decision caused it.
5. Give the verdict: keep, adjust or stop, and the next step.

## Keep the states apart

- **Recommended:** the brief was written.
- **Acted on:** the merchant reports doing it. Record their words; the skill cannot see a PO it did not create.
- **Reviewed:** the outcome and guardrail were measured over the window.

A brief that was recommended but never reviewed has no proven impact. Say so rather than assume it worked.
