# Slow stock: what do I do with too much?

The decision serves Cash, and its guardrail is Availability. This guide recommends; it does not cancel purchase orders, change prices or edit settings. The merchant acts.

## When stock counts as slow

Flag a variant when its days of inventory exceed lead time + order cycle + safety days + 60. That is more than the stock needs to last until the next order would arrive, plus two months.

- When those settings are unknown, flag over 90 days.
- Over 60 days is worth a look: it is the band many tools show as possible overstock.
- When receipt dates exist, also show the age of the stock in brackets of 30 / 60 / 90+ days.

## Rule out first

- **It kept selling out:** an item that is "slow" because it was rarely in stock has an Availability problem, not a Cash one. Check censored periods.
- **Off-season:** a seasonal item in its off-season, with a known season ahead, is not dead. Compare with the same period last year.
- **A seasonal pre-build:** stock bought ahead of a peak is meant to sit. Judge it by end-of-season sell-through instead.
- **A new item:** too young to judge. Use days since first sale.
- **Bundle or kit stock:** a component may be held for bundles. Count bundle sales on the component.
- **A leftover override:** a high order cycle or fixed order quantity, set once for a bulk buy and never removed, keeps reordering too much. Fix the setting as well as the stock.

## Options, in order

1. **Stop buying:** exclude the variant from replenishment, and cancel draft or open POs that have not shipped.
2. **Cut order quantities** for the future, if the item should stay in the range.
3. **Supplier return,** where the supplier's terms allow it.
4. **Move it** to a location where it sells.
5. **Bundle with a fast-seller.**
6. **Mark down part** of the stock, not all of it, and keep what the coming season needs.
7. **Liquidate or write off** what cannot sell at any reasonable price.

Earlier options cost less margin. Move down the list only when the earlier ones are not enough to reach the cash target.

## Keep enough

Keep the forecast cover for the coming season at full price. Clear only the excess above it.

## Guardrail

- Availability for the kept quantity: no new sold-out days in the season.
- Margin on the cleared units stays above zero, or the merchant has accepted a loss knowingly.

## Review

Review after 60–90 days. Slow stock moves slowly, so 30 days rarely shows a change.

## Worked example (fictional)

A merchant has 400 Canvas Totes in Sand that "barely move".

- **Cash at stake:** 400 × $8 cost = $3,200.
- **Rate:** 12 a week now, so about 33 weeks of cover, well over the flag.
- **Ruled out:** it never sold out. It is off-season, though: last spring it sold 30 a week from March to June.
- **Found:** a draft PO for 100 more totes.
- **Do this:** cancel the draft. Bundle the tote with the Trail Sock, and mark down 150 units by 20%. Keep 250, about 8 weeks of spring demand.
- **Ruled out:** clearing all 400, which gives up spring sales at full price.
- **Guardrail:** keep at least 8 weeks of spring cover, and margin on the cleared units above zero.
- **Look again** in 60 days. Keep if slow-stock value is down to about $2,000.
