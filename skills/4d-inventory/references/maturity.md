# Maturity: pitch the advice to how the merchant decides today

Four levels, from the Stock Balance model. Use them to choose what advice will help; the weakest pillar comes first whatever the level.

| Level | How they decide | Signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Gut feel | Reorder when shelves look low | No reorder point; "I just know"; frequent sell-outs or piles of stock |
| 2. Spreadsheet | A sheet of sales and stock, updated by hand | Numbers are right but stale; one person owns the sheet |
| 3. Rule-based | Min/max or reorder points in a tool | Rules that never adapt; lead times typed once and never checked |
| 4. Forecast-driven | Forecasts generate suggestions for review | A forecast tool in use; questions about accuracy and ranges |

Ask one question to place them if the conversation has not already shown it: "How do you decide today when and how much to reorder?"

## Advice per level

- **Level 1–2:** one rule to adopt. A reorder point from lead time × sales speed, plus safety stock in days of cover. Show it worked on one of their variants.
- **Level 3:** make the rule adapt.
  - Use the observed lead time, not the typed one.
  - Compute sales speed without sold-out days.
  - Tier safety stock by ABC class.
  - Set the review cadence by sales speed: daily for items selling over 5/day, weekly at 1–5/day, every two weeks under 1/day.
- **Level 4:** check the forecast itself: whether about 80% of actuals fall inside its range, whether it runs high or low (bias), and whether it beats a simple baseline (MASE).

## What this is not

This skill never scores the person. The level only decides which advice is useful now. The 4D framework assesses proficiency separately, from observed human behaviour, and a good brief written with AI help says nothing about the merchant's own competence.
