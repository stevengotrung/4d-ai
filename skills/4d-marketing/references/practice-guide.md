# Marketing & Growth Experiments: practice guide

## Experiment specification

Record audience, hypothesis, variant/control, assignment or comparison method, metric definitions and denominators, conversion window, duration or sample rationale, cost limits, guardrails, owner, and result location. For low-volume work, an explicitly exploratory test may be more honest than a claim of statistical proof. Consult an appropriate analysis method rather than inventing a universal significance threshold.

## Creative and claims

Tie claims to supplied evidence and current offer terms. Keep unverifiable claims out of publication-ready copy, or clearly flag them for validation. Follow brand and channel constraints without inventing them. Regulatory or policy questions needing current authoritative guidance should be verified through available trusted sources or routed to the responsible reviewer.

## Worked example (fictional)

**Input:** CTR doubles while qualified leads fall at unchanged spend. The team asks to scale the winning creative, but qualification lag and traffic mix have not been checked.

**Avoid:** Calling the creative a winner from clicks and increasing spend automatically.

**Reasoned response:** Check the conversion/qualification window and tracking, compare qualified outcomes at comparable exposure, and investigate whether the new message attracts unsuitable traffic. Propose a bounded test; draft variants only from verified product claims.

**Example artifact:** Hypothesis: the new message attracts more clicks but less relevant prospects. Needed: cohort-level qualification outcomes after the full lag window. Proposed test: compare a clearer audience-specific message against the existing version. Primary outcome: qualified leads per spend; guardrails: total cost and sales follow-up burden. No budget change authorized.

**Follow-up:** Review comparable mature cohorts, data completeness, and changes in qualification rules before deciding to scale, revise, or stop.
