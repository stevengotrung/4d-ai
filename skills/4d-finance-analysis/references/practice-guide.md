# Finance & Business Analysis: practice guide

## Reconciliation before explanation

Document entity/perimeter, period, currency, unit, gross/net treatment, source versions, and report definitions. Check opening + movements = closing where that identity applies. Disclose excluded records and rounding. Do not conflate arithmetic consistency with source accuracy or audit assurance.

## Variance bridges and scenarios

Use an explicit reproducible method. Explain volume, rate/price, mix, timing, and one-off effects only where data supports the decomposition; define the convention so interacting effects are not counted twice. Preserve unexplained residuals and plan the next check. Scenarios should label assumptions, horizon, costs, sensitivity, and limitations. Forecasts are conditional, not guaranteed.

## Outputs and authority

A concise narrative may be enough. If a spreadsheet or model is created, preserve input/calculation/output separation, units, provenance, and meaningful formula checks. Use available artifact tools when warranted, but do not require a spreadsheet to answer a simple question. Unknown specialist treatment belongs with a qualified reviewer, not an invented rule. No transaction or statutory action is authorized by an analysis request.

## Worked example (fictional)

**Input:** Revenue rises while operating cash falls. The supplied data suggests slower collections, but inventory payments, reporting periods, and one-time transactions have not been reconciled.

**Response:** Align periods and definitions, reconcile available cash and revenue records, and distinguish supported movements from competing explanations. Do not claim profit equals cash or attribute the entire change to receivables without a bridge.

**Draft artifact:** Question: what explains the operating cash change? Known: revenue is higher under the supplied report definition. Needed: consistent period balances and cash-flow detail. Draft bridge: supported collections, supplier payments, and other identified movements; unreconciled amounts remain labeled. No invented balancing item or accounting entry.

**Follow-up:** Repeat the reconciled comparison after the agreed reporting period. Track whether expected collection or payment changes occurred and whether one-time effects explain the result. Keep model assumptions separate from actual benefits.

This example applies the [4D Framework](https://4dframework.org/), a proposed competency model. It is fictional, not evidence of verified results or professional certification.
