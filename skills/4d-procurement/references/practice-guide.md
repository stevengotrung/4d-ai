# Procurement & Supplier Evaluation: practice guide

## Comparable quotations

Record quote version/date, validity, currency, units, quantities, scope, exclusions, payment terms and approval limits. Do not silently combine unlike specifications or assume current exchange rates. Use provided dated rates for a clearly labeled scenario; verify current rates when a live decision needs them. Tax, legal, and compliance questions need authoritative context or qualified review.

## Cost and uncertainty

Show inputs and formula for relevant total cost, and label hypothetical scenarios. Use alternative assumptions for material unknowns instead of a single precise winner. Distinguish costs per ordered unit, accepted usable unit, and consumption period. Do not count a cost twice or omit supplier-switching work solely because it is not on the quote.

## Scope with inventory

This skill evaluates sourcing options and supplier terms. Forecast trust, replenishment quantity, or slow-stock decisions are separate. Do not duplicate or override inventory-specific calculations or restrictions. A handoff can contain the supply conditions already verified; no inventory skill is required to compare quotations.

## Worked example (fictional)

**Input:** Supplier B offers a lower unit price but a larger minimum order, an unverified delivery promise, and a different specification. Warehouse capacity is limited.

**Response:** Normalize the quotations against the required specification and quantity. Keep the specification difference and unverified lead time visible; compare cost at a justified demand volume, not just price per purchased unit. Propose a limited sample or trial if the owner approves.

**Draft artifact:** Decision: whether to trial Supplier B. Open gates: specification equivalence, delivery evidence, storage capacity. Known: lower unit price and larger commitment. Unknown: rejection/rework cost. Recommendation: verify critical requirements before switching. No purchase order or supplier acceptance created.

**Follow-up:** Record accepted quality, on-time/full delivery, actual total cost and usable output for a comparable trial. Review after the agreed receipt/service window before recommending expansion.

This example applies the [4D Framework](https://4dframework.org/), a proposed competency model. It is fictional, not evidence of verified results or professional certification.
